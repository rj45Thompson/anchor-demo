How the number is computed

Count how many independent sources assert the fact. That count maps to a probability through a curve fitted against a held-out source the system never consults when answering.

Fitted on one half of the entities and tested on the other, split by subject so no entity appears on both sides. Expected calibration error: 0.20 percentage points across 496,464 held-out facts. The curve predicts — it is not describing its own training data.

The law

An anchor is a second source, not a second opinion. One source is close to a coin flip. A second independently collected source takes it to 87%. That lift survives controls for popularity, for shared lineage between sources, and for gaps in the reference set.

What this is. The whole graph — 66,404,117 facts over 12.2 million subjects — sharded so your browser fetches only the piece it needs. It is a lookup over recorded relations, not a chatbot: it reports what sources asserted, and when nothing asserts anything it says so instead of inventing.

Two limits, stated rather than hidden. (1) Subjects are matched by surface text, not a disambiguated entity id, so a name shared by more than one real thing (many towns are named "Berlin") can appear merged under one heading. Every fact shown is still real and independently sourced — it may just belong to more than one thing with that name. (2) There is no per-subject cap: every one of the 66,404,117 facts is here, and a well-documented subject keeps all of its edges (Australia carries 975). An earlier build capped each subject at 80 facts and, by doing so, silently deleted Australia → capital  → Canberra — the answer to this page's own headline example. Measuring the cap rather than defending it is what removed it: it was discarding 1.58% of the graph, and 99.9% of subjects never came near it. "Not recorded here" now genuinely means the sources did not record it.

A lookup answers one hop. This chains them, hop by hop, over 66 million facts — and when it cannot reach the target it abstains, goes and learns what else that thing is called, and tries again.

What just happened

Ask something and this panel will explain, in plain English, what the engine actually did for that question — how it read it, what it opened, what completed, and what the numbers mean. Nothing here is pre-written; it is generated from the same counters the trace shows.

Hand-written: a listed table of about fifteen question-word aliases, so "who founded X" reaches founded by. Nothing about any specific subject is special-cased and no answer is stored. A "no chain" result is not proof there is none — the trace shows exactly what was and was not opened.

The other half of the same idea, in a different domain: given input→output examples, search a fixed set of primitives for one that satisfies every example — verified by real execution, not predicted by a model. This box re-implements a small slice of it entirely in this page; the measured system is a ~2,600-line search engine tested against 224 MBPP+ programming tasks.

No model runs here. The facts panel hashes your subject, fetches one shard, and counts sources in your browser.
