A trading script, finished and tested. The original arrived cut off mid-line at the PUT trigger check (elif last_price < orb_lo); every change from it is listed below, and every value in zdt_autopilot.py that is not from the original says so beside it.

Run it on a paper account first

  1. Start TWS (or IB Gateway) logged in to the PAPER account. In TWS: Global Configuration, API, Settings: tick "Enable ActiveX and Socket Clients", port 7497.
  2. The account needs live market data for SPY and for US options (OPRA). Without it the bot stops at startup and says so.
  3. pip install -r requirements.txt
  4. py zdt_autopilot.py

The bot refuses a live port (7496, 4001) or a live account (IBKR paper account IDs start with "D") unless you add --live.

It sends no orders until the two exit rules are set (STOP_LOSS_PCT, TAKE_PROFIT_PCT near the top of the file). The original stops before its exit rules, so they are not known. Until they are set it runs SIGNALS ONLY: it does everything else (opening range, signals, the contract it would buy, how many) and prints the order it would send.

The use case it follows

Actor: the trader, running the script on their own PC beside TWS or IB Gateway. Goal: on a trading day, buy a SPY 0DTE option when SPY breaks out of its first 15-minute range, sized to the balance, let IB hold the exits, write every trade to the CSV, and never leave an order or a position behind. Preconditions: TWS or IB Gateway is running with the API on PORT; the account has live SPY and OPRA data.

Main path, each step with what you see:

  1. You start the script. It connects and prints PAPER or LIVE with the account ID.
  2. It reads the account's USD TotalCashBalance (the original's field) and its own budget: STARTING_BUDGET_USD ($100) before its first trade, then the last ending balance in its log. It prints [ACCOUNT] and [BUDGET].
  3. It reads today's session hours from IB, waits for the first 15-minute bar to close, and reads that bar. It prints [ORB ESTABLISHED] and the two triggers (high + ORB_BUFFER, low - ORB_BUFFER).
  4. It streams SPY's last price and prints a status line every second.
  5. SPY trades above the CALL trigger or below the PUT trigger. It prints [SIGNAL].
  6. It picks today's expiry and the listed strike nearest spot +/- 1.50 rounded to the dollar (the original's rule), always out of the money, a tie going to the one nearer spot +/- 1.50. It prints [CONTRACT].
  7. It reads that option's ask and sizes the order with the original's tier table, from its budget and never from more than the account's cash. It prints [ORDER].
  8. It buys with a limit at the ask and waits for the fill. It prints [FILLED].
  9. It places the stop and the target at IB as one OCA pair, so they stay working even if the PC or the script dies. It prints [EXITS AT IB].
  10. One exit fills, or the flatten time arrives (10 minutes before the close) and it sells at market. It prints [TRADE CLOSED].
  11. It writes one row to trade_execution_log.csv in the original's columns, with the net P/L after the commissions IB reports.
  12. It waits for SPY to come back between the triggers before it takes another signal, and stops for the day after MAX_CONSECUTIVE_LOSSES losses in a row. It prints [REARMED] or [CIRCUIT BREAKER].
  13. At the flatten time, or on Ctrl+C, it cancels its own orders, sells what it bought, and disconnects.

Alternate paths:

  • 1a. Live port or live account without --live: it refuses and exits.
  • 2a. IB reports no USD TotalCashBalance: it stops. The original carried on with $100 instead.
  • 2b. The log's last row says UNREPORTED: the budget is not known, and it stops.
  • 3a. Started after 9:45 ET: it still reads the 9:30 bar. The original read the latest 30 minutes, so after about 10:15 its "opening range" was a later bar, and before 9:45 it was a bar still forming.
  • 3b. IB lists today as CLOSED: it does nothing. A half day closes early, and the flatten time moves with it.
  • 4a. No live SPY price within 15 seconds, or the data is delayed: it stops and says why.
  • 6a. Today's expiry is not listed: no trade today. The original switched to the next expiry, which is not a 0DTE trade.
  • 6b. No out-of-the-money strike within $5 of the target is listed: no trade today. The original crashed here (min() of an empty list), and it never checked that IB knew the contract it picked.
  • 7a. The option has no live bid and ask: this signal is skipped.
  • 7b. One contract costs more than the budget, or than the cash: no trade today. The original bought one anyway.
  • 8a. Not filled within 10 seconds: the rest is cancelled and only what filled is traded.
  • 9a. Exit rules not set: SIGNALS ONLY, no orders at all.
  • 10a. The connection drops while holding: the stop and target keep working at IB; the script says what is still open.
  • 11a. IB never sends the commission: the row says UNREPORTED rather than an estimate, and the bot stops for the day because the balance is no longer known.

What is from the original and what is new

Kept as the original wrote it: the configuration, the CSV columns, the tier table, the strike rule (spot +/- 1.50, nearest within $5), the ORB buffer and triggers, the consecutive-loss breaker, the prints.

Changed, each one a defect in the original as it arrived:

  • get_safe_tier_contracts returned max(1, cts), so it bought one contract it could not afford. It now returns 0 and the bot does not trade (7b).
  • The opening range was bars[0] of the last 30 minutes, which is the opening bar only between about 9:45 and 10:15 (3a).
  • When the balance could not be read it used STARTING_BUDGET_USD ($100) as if it were real (2a).
  • When today's expiry was missing it traded the next one (6a).
  • An empty strike list crashed it, and neither qualifyContracts call was checked. ib_async reports a failure as None inside a non-empty list, so the result has to be read, not just called (6b).
  • The date came from the PC's clock in its own time zone; it now comes from New York time.

New, because the file ends before them: steps 7 to 13, the live guard (1a), the session hours (3b), the re-arm rule (12), and the cleanup (13). Every value that is not from the original is marked in the file.

From the owner's answer (2026-11-08): start it with $100 and trade 0DTE within that budget, buying cheap calls when the market is going up and cheap puts when it is going down. The bot now sizes from its own budget, STARTING_BUDGET_USD to start and the log's last ending balance after that, and never from more than the account holds (2, 7, 2b). Calls on an up move and puts on a down move is the breakout rule it already had (5).

The light loop

spec_check/ holds a check of the code against a written spec, spec_check/SPEC.md: the use case above, the original script's own comments, and ib_async's documentation, each copied word for word. Every rule in rules.json quotes it, every suspect in suspects.json names a rule and a code line, and a fault counts only when that suspect's repro (repros/) fails its own assertion on the code.

It now holds 19 rules and 18 suspects. The first run proved 3 faults (REPORT_before_fixes.md): a tie between two listed strikes went to the one farther from spot +/- 1.50; with the strikes just above spot unlisted it could buy an in-the-money call, against the original's "slightly OTM"; and Ctrl+C while waiting for IB's commission report left a closed trade out of the CSV. All three are fixed and each has a test. The second run proved none (REPORT.md).

Run any repro on its own: python spec_check/run_repro.py S1.

Epics and use cases

model/ is the bot written as two epics: trading one day (seven use cases) and getting it ready for real money (four). Each use case gives its actor, trigger, success and minimal guarantee, its steps, and every alternate path with where it leads. Requirements (model/requirements.yaml) never name code: each is carried by steps, and only the steps name code. model/step_tests.yaml names the tests that run each step and path; py tests/step_trace.py runs them under a tracer and writes the code each one ran (model/step_trace.json), and tests/test_model.py fails any step whose named code its tests never ran. py make_epics_page.py OUT writes the page that shows it all. The model also passes the closed-loop kit's use case checker (usecase_model.py, rules U1 to U19) with no holes; set USECASE_MODEL to that file and tests/test_model.py runs it too.

Tests

pip install -r requirements-dev.txt, then py -m pytest tests (84 tests) runs the bot through whole simulated trading days against an in-memory TWS (tests/fake_ib_async.py), with tests for every step and alternate path of the trading epic. No TWS, network or account is involved. tests/test_api_contract.py reads the real ib_async source and checks that every ib. call, keyword and field the bot uses exists there. It runs when ib_async is installed, or when IB_ASYNC_SRC points at its source, and is skipped otherwise.

py tests/plant_check.py plants 30 defects, one at a time, in copies of the bot and of the model (the original's five among them), and each must make a test fail. A check that has only ever passed cannot be told from one that is switched off.

What the tests cannot show: how IB itself fills, prices and reports on a given day. The first paper run is that check.

py tests/demo_day.py prints one simulated day the way the terminal shows it (tests/demo_day_signals_only.txt); py tests/demo_day.py --exits 0.5 1.0 does the same with a stop and a target set, test values only (tests/demo_day_with_exits.txt).

Replay on real sessions

sim/ runs the bot's own code, unchanged, through SPY's real one-minute bars: py sim/market_data.py fetches the latest five sessions (Yahoo Finance's public chart data), py sim/replay.py replays them with the budget carried day to day (--reset-daily starts each at $100; --stop and --target set the test exits), and py sim/make_replay_page.py OUT writes the page that plays each day back. Option prices there are modeled (Black-Scholes, VIX as the volatility, the time left to the close), because past 0DTE quotes are not in free data; sim/README.md says what is real and what is modeled. The replay has its own light loop (sim/spec_check/): six rules from its use case, among them that no decision uses a price from the future, none broken, and a planted look-ahead caught.

How others do it

Searched on 2026-11-08. Each item links to a public page; where that page blocks readers, a readable summary is linked beside it. This is what others built and what their authors report, not advice.

On Hugging Face

A search of Hugging Face's model, dataset and Space names for "0dte", "opening range breakout" and "spy options" finds no model or dataset for this strategy: three Spaces have 0DTE in their names, and the two datasets it matches are unrelated. The nearest data:

  • paperswithbacktest/Options-Daily-Price: daily bars of every US-listed option that traded since June 2014, index weeklies included, with implied volatility and greeks. Daily bars cannot test a 9:45 entry. Access needs the owner's approval.
  • fabhaus/equities_5m_options: 5-minute bars for options on about 13,300 US underlyings, January 2024 to March 2026. Fine enough for an intraday replay; its card does not list the underlyings, so check that SPY is among them first.

Tools that run this kind of bot

  • ib_async, the library this bot uses. It replaces ib_insync, which many examples online still use.
  • QuantConnect LEAN filters an option chain to today's expiry with expiration(0, 0) and picks strikes by delta with delta(min, max). It trades equity and index options live through Interactive Brokers, with stop, limit, bracket and one-cancels-other orders.
  • NautilusTrader: its Interactive Brokers adapter trades through TWS or IB Gateway and loads option chains for a window of days from today.

The same signal in research

  • Zarattini and Aziz (2023), Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable? (summary): QQQ shares, a 5-minute opening range, entered in the direction of the first bar, the stop at that bar's other end, the target 10 times the risk, out at the close. They report 33.2% a year against 14.9% for holding QQQ, January 2016 to February 2023, with commission charged but no bid-ask spread or slippage.
  • Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz (2024), A Profitable Day Trading Strategy For The U.S. Equity Market: the 5-minute range again (15, 30 and 60 minutes compared), traded only in stocks far more active than usual that day, 2016 to 2023. They report a net Sharpe ratio of 2.81 for the top 20.
  • Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon (2024), Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P500 ETF (SPY): trend entries on SPY when intraday demand looks abnormal, with trailing stops, 2007 to early 2024. They report 19.6% a year net of costs. The abstract does not mention options.
  • Option Alpha (2025), Opening range breakout 0DTE options: the breakout on SPX, but on a break it sells a 0DTE credit spread (a put spread on an upside break) and holds it to the close. Of the 15, 30 and 60-minute ranges it tested, its bot uses the 60-minute one.

0DTE options and their costs in research

  • Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda (2023), Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They? (summary): retail traders lost about $70 million in all on 0DTE S&P 500 index options, January 2021 to February 2023, and their average daily net loss rose from $184,000 to $358,000 after daily expiries began in May 2022.
  • Almeida, Freire and Hizmeri (draft of May 2025), 0DTE Asset Pricing: 0DTE options are now about half of all S&P 500 option volume. In them, investors are paid mainly for bearing the risk of the market rising rather than falling, and most 0DTEs look mispriced to a risk-averse investor; that was highly profitable to exploit before 2022 and faded once 0DTEs were listed every day.
  • Vilkov, 0DTE Trading Rules: Tail Risk, Implementation, and Tactical Timing, with its code at vilkovgr/0dte-strategies: rule-based 0DTE option strategies. Its August 2026 correction says the bid-ask half-spread had been charged at 1/100 of its true size; with that fixed, no strategy or basket keeps a positive net Sharpe ratio.

AI agents that trade

  • TradingAgents: a team of language-model agents (analysts, bull and bear researchers, a trader, risk managers, a portfolio manager) deciding trades in stocks, ETFs and crypto. It runs on Claude among many models, sends its orders to a simulated exchange, and says it is for research.
  • Li, Kim, Cucuringu and Ma (2025), Can LLM-based Financial Investing Strategies Outperform the Market in Long Run?: over two decades and more than 100 symbols, the advantages earlier reported for LLM strategies shrink a lot; the strategies were too cautious in rising markets and too aggressive in falling ones.
  • Alpaca's MCP server lets Claude (Desktop or Code) and other assistants place single and multi-leg option orders at the broker Alpaca, on a paper account unless switched off. A different broker, and no strategy of its own.

How this bot differs

  • Its range is the first 15 minutes and it enters $0.30 past it (ORB_BUFFER). The papers above use a 5-minute range, compare 15, 30 and 60, and add filters such as trading only unusually active stocks.
  • It buys one option a fixed $1.50 out of the money (about 0.20 delta, the original's comment says). QuantConnect picks strikes by delta (open question 6), and Option Alpha's 0DTE version sells a spread instead of buying.
  • The breakout papers trade shares and report gains. The 0DTE studies above report retail traders losing money, and Vilkov's net results reversed once the spread was charged at its true size. The bot's replay prices options with a model, so a paper run on real quotes is the check that counts.
