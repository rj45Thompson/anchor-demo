The key is accuracy

Accuracy boosts productivity across the board. In our view it makes AI, in some cases, thousands of times smarter. That multiplier is our view, not a measurement: the largest multiplier we have measured is 216 times less reading.

The gap between what AI guesses and what is true is what people deem AI slop. At TNT Tools the standard is no slop: every value is either read from a source, with a note of where, or marked absent with the reason. There is no third kind.

Where these numbers come from: our knowledge graph, on facts

Measured 99.03% of 35,951 derivations it had never seen were right, using reasoning rules guarded by type, on a verified-or-abstain knowledge graph of 60.33 million facts, each carrying which sources state it.

Measured 616 of 616 of its top-tier facts were right against an outside truth (cached Wikidata), on a frozen test population of 2,714 entities, with the bars declared before the run.

Measured 99.2% right on 209 held-out questions, answering 61% of them and abstaining on the rest. Asked the same questions on the same facts, a local language model answered 95% at 98.0%, with 4 confident wrong answers. The accuracy comes from being allowed to say "I don't know".

Those are facts, not code. On code, the numbers are in one feature over five passes and six kinds of task, and the target for agents writing code with the loop is still a target. Graders on those tasks counted 17 invented values with the loop and 5 without, so "no guesses" is the standard being worked toward there, and the count is how we know.
